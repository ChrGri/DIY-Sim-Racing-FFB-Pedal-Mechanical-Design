5 · Recommendations

What this means for the design

  • The joint position works. With the link at the height of the plate center, the lower arm carries almost pure compression and very little bending.
  • Fillet the inside edge between side rail and cross brace (R 3–5 mm). The stress peak sits there in every load case. A fillet lowers the notch stress considerably and adds no weight.
  • Torsion is the weakest load case. The lower arm is an open U-section. A continuous back wall or a second cross brace further up would close it into a box and greatly reduce torsional stress and twist, if there is room for it.
  • Mind the print orientation. Orient the side rails so their length lies in the layer plane. Then the higher strength applies to bending and torsion stresses.
  • Stiffness is not an issue. 0.02–0.6 mm per 1000 N is small compared to pedal travel and to the compliance of the TPE adapters.
6 · Limits

What the analysis does not cover

  • The print is treated as homogeneous. Perimeters, infill and layer lines are not modeled individually; the strengths are assumptions.
  • Linear-elastic and static: no fatigue, no creep, no contact pressure at screws and bearing seats.
  • The faceplate is not included. The force is applied as pressure directly on the arm's contact face.
  • Peak values at sharp edges depend on the mesh. A reliable notch stress would need local mesh refinement there.
